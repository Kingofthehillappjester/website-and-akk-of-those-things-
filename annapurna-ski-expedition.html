Safety at Altitude

Ski touring at 4,000–5,416m
is a serious undertaking.
We do not minimise this.

This is the first ski tour ever run in this part of the Annapurna region with a group of this size, and we treat it accordingly. Safety is not one consideration among many — it is the condition on which everything else depends. From the moment you arrive, the guides’ word is final. When they say go, we go; when they say stop, wait or turn back, we do so without debate. That single discipline — everyone listening to their guides, without exception — is what makes an undertaking like this possible at all.

Every decision that carries risk — where we ski, when we move, whether the high crossing goes ahead — is made by people who have operated in this terrain for over a decade, backed by a dedicated medical team monitoring the whole group every day. We would rather stand a guest down for a day than let ambition override judgement. Nobody is ever pushed beyond what the guides and doctors assess as safe on the day.

Weather & Conditions

All skiing is weather and conditions dependent. Most skiing is done in the morning, when the snowpack is at its most stable — avalanche risk rises through the day as the snow heats on the mountain. The HST guide team assesses snowpack, terrain and weather daily, and has the final decision on where, when and whether skiing takes place. Where conditions are not safe, plans are adapted or paused. No descent, session or high-point attempt goes ahead without the guides’ sign-off.

Acclimatisation

Staged ascent: Kathmandu, Pokhara, Jomsom (2,720m), Muktinath (3,710m, two nights) — four days before base camp.

Doctors On Site

Three trained doctors are on site at all times, dedicated to recognising and managing altitude sickness. The whole group is monitored daily — symptoms are caught early, and any guest showing signs is treated or stood down before it becomes serious. The medical team and guides make the call together.

Medical & Evacuation

Pulse oximeters, altitude medication and a full medical kit on site. Pre-established emergency evacuation protocols with Pokhara helicopter rescue, briefed and rehearsed before anyone goes high.

Avalanche Safety

Transceiver, probe and shovel mandatory. Full kit briefing Day 1. Routes assessed by HST guide team each morning based on conditions.

Fitness & Ability

Comfortable on red and black runs is the benchmark. No touring experience required — snowmobile uplift is available after the first tour. 12-week prep guide with every booking.

Permits & Licences

NTB Trekking Agency Licence, TAAN member. All permits included: ACAP, TIMS, and Restricted Area Permit for upper Mustang.

What To Bring

Your kit list.
Bring it with you.

This expedition operates at extreme altitude in temperatures that regularly reach −20°C to −25°C. Your personal equipment is your responsibility and must be brought with you. Everything below is what you need to be safe, warm and able to ski for ten days on the mountain.

Critical — Read First

You must bring your own skis (or splitboard) and your own ski boots. There are no ski shops anywhere in Nepal — nothing can be bought, hired or replaced once you arrive. Travel with your ski and boot bag as checked luggage and do not leave it to chance. If you do not own suitable equipment, contact us before you book (see below).

Skis / Splitboard

Ski touring skis with touring bindings and climbing skins, or a splitboard with skins. Bring your own — no exceptions. Skiers: bring your own boots. Snowboarders: bring your own boots.

Avalanche Safety

Avalanche transceiver (beacon), probe and shovel — all mandatory. An ABS / airbag backpack is required. These must be your own or arranged in advance.

Outer Layers

Ski jacket and salopettes / bib rated for −20°C to −25°C. Waterproof, windproof and genuinely insulated for extreme cold and high altitude.

Mid Layers

Insulated mid layers — down or synthetic jacket, fleece or softshell. Layering is how you manage temperature swings between skinning and standing still.

Base Layers

Merino or technical thermal base layers, top and bottom. Bring several sets. Cotton has no place at this altitude.

Hands

A good pair of insulated ski gloves or mittens, plus liner (under) gloves. Spares strongly recommended — cold hands end days early.

Head & Eyes

Ski helmet, ski goggles (with a low-light lens option), and category 4 glacier sunglasses for the intense high-altitude glare.

Feet

Technical ski socks (several pairs) and warm camp / down booties for evenings at base camp. Keep a dry set in reserve.

Extremities & Extras

Warm hat and neck gaiter / buff, sun cream and lip balm (high SPF), and a personal water bottle or flask. Any personal medication for the full trip.

Don’t have everything?
We can help. For guests who don’t already own suitable equipment, we can arrange kit — including ski touring setups and avalanche safety gear — for an additional charge, organised in advance of departure. Before You Book

Questions, answered honestly.

The things guests ask most often before committing. We would rather you knew exactly what you are signing up for.

What skiing level do I need?

You should be comfortable on red and black runs and have skied off-piste before. You do not need to have ski-toured before — touring is straightforward to pick up, the terrain here is forgiving for first-timers, and the guides will get you up to speed. What matters is that you arrive a confident off-piste skier and willing to learn.

Do I need to have ski-toured before?

No. Plenty of guests come to this without touring experience. It is an easy skill to learn and the terrain is well suited to learning it. If you would rather not tour at all on a given day, snowmobile uplift is available after the first tour of the morning.

Who else is on the trip — what’s the age range?

Guests typically range from late twenties to mid-fifties. It is a group of capable, like-minded people rather than a narrow age bracket.

I’m travelling solo — is that a problem?

Not at all — solo travellers are very welcome. Every tent is single-occupancy, so you have your own private space with no sharing and no single supplement. Tents have double beds, so couples who want to share a tent can do so.

What happens if I get altitude sickness?

We monitor the whole group daily and three trained doctors are on site at all times specifically for altitude illness. If the medical team and guides decide you need to descend or be evacuated, that decision is final and there is nothing we can do to keep you on the mountain — it is a genuine risk of high-altitude travel that you accept by joining. That said, if you complete the preparation programme beforehand, or have been to altitude before, you should be well placed to cope. This is exactly why comprehensive high-altitude and evacuation insurance is mandatory (see below).

Is insurance required?

Yes — it is mandatory. You must hold travel insurance that explicitly covers high-altitude trekking in Nepal, emergency medical treatment, and helicopter evacuation, on top of standard travel cover. We will ask for proof before departure and cannot take you on the expedition without it.

Are flights included?

No. International flights are not included from anywhere in the world to Kathmandu. Everything from your arrival in Kathmandu onwards — hotels, base camp, all food and drink, internal transfers, permits, guides and the medical team — is covered.

What if the weather disrupts the trip?

Weather disruption is not something to worry about, but big storms can occur in winter in the Muktinath valley, and we plan for them. We do everything in our power to keep the itinerary on track. If the helicopters on the final day are delayed or grounded, we have vehicles ready to drive you to Pokhara or all the way to Kathmandu. For this reason we recommend booking your onward international flight for the day after the helicopter exit, not the same evening, so you always have enough time to get out.
