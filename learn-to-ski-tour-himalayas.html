The Trip

Rugged by design.
Easy to start.

This is the way in. You don’t need to have toured before. You need to be comfortable on reds and blacks and happy in soft snow off the piste. HST’s guides teach everything else, step by step, before you ski anything serious.

It’s rugged on purpose. Nights are in teahouse lodges with simple twin rooms and shared facilities, the food is hearty Nepali and Western cooking, and porters carry your skis and kit bag. In return you get the Sanctuary itself, one of the great amphitheatres of the Himalaya, and the skills to come back for bigger things.

This is for you if…

  • You ski reds and blacks with confidence
  • You’ve skied some off-piste, even just soft snow beside the piste
  • You can walk five to seven hours a day, several days in a row
  • You’d rather have the mountain than the minibar

It’s not for you if…

  • You’ve never left the piste — take an off-piste course first
  • You want five-star hotels every night — see the Annapurna Ski Expedition
  • You already tour confidently on glaciers — look at Mera Peak

The walk in

A short drive, then five days on foot through terraced Gurung villages, bamboo and rhododendron forest, climbing steadily via Chhomrong and Deurali to Machhapuchhre Base Camp. Porters carry the skis; you carry a day pack. The section above Deurali is checked every morning for avalanche risk, and we only move through it when the guides are happy.

Days 8–1103

Four days of learning to tour

Annapurna Base Camp sits at 4,130m in a bowl ringed by Annapurna I, Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre. Day one is skins, kick turns, transitions and avalanche rescue drills. Then each day goes a little further: longer skins, steeper ground and your first real Himalayan descents, always on slopes the guides have assessed that morning.

Before You Book

What you’ll need.

Ski level

Confident on reds and blacks, with some off-piste. No touring experience needed: that’s what the trip is for.

Fitness

Five to seven hours walking a day on the way in, then touring at over 4,000m. We send a training plan when you book.

Kit

Touring skis or a splitboard, skins, boots, transceiver, probe, shovel and helmet. There are no ski shops in Nepal, so bring yours or let us arrange it before you fly.

Weather & conditions

All skiing depends on snow and weather. The guides assess the route and snowpack every morning and have the final say on where and whether we ski. Some days the best lesson is waiting.

Questions

Asked, and answered honestly.

I’ve never ski toured. Is that really OK?

Yes. That’s exactly who this trip is for. If you’re a confident skier on reds and blacks and you’ve had a taste of off-piste, the guides will teach you to skin, turn and transition before you ski anything committing.

What are teahouses like?

Simple mountain lodges run by local families: twin rooms, shared bathrooms, a warm dining room with a stove, and big plates of food. Hot showers and charging are usually available for a small charge. It’s comfortable, not luxurious.

Can I hire skis in Nepal?

No. There are no ski shops in Nepal, so nothing can be hired once you arrive. Bring your own touring setup, or tell us when you book and we’ll arrange touring and avalanche kit for you in advance.

How cold is it?

Nights in the Sanctuary are well below freezing, and sunny days can feel surprisingly warm. Layering is everything. We send a full kit list with your booking.

What comes after this trip?

Once you can tour, the next step up is the Mera Peak Ski Expedition, a 6,476m summit on skis. Or take the luxury route over Thorong La on the Annapurna Ski Expedition.
