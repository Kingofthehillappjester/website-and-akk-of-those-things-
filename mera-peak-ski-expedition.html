The Expedition

A real summit.
A rugged trip.

Mera is Nepal’s highest trekking peak, and one of the few 6,000-metre summits you can realistically climb with skis. The route is a long glacier climb rather than a technical one, but at this height everything is hard work, and whether you ski from the top depends entirely on the snow that day.

It’s rugged by design: teahouse lodges on the way in, tents at base camp and high camp, and long days. You need to be a confident off-piste skier who has toured before. Our Learn to Ski Tour trip counts.

This is for you if…

  • You ski confidently off-piste in steep and variable snow
  • You’ve ski toured before, on our Learn to Tour trip or elsewhere
  • You’re fit for long days with a pack above 5,000m
  • You’re happy camping in the cold

It’s not for you if…

  • You’ve never toured — start with Learn to Ski Tour
  • You want luxury — the Annapurna Ski Expedition is our luxury trip
  • You need a guaranteed ski from the summit — on Mera, the snow decides

The long way round, on purpose

Rather than rushing over the high Zatrwa La pass on the first days, we take a slower approach into the Hinku valley. It adds days, but it lets you acclimatise properly. Forest and yak pastures give way to moraine and ice as you climb through Kothe and Thaknak to Khare at about 5,045m.

Before You Book

What you’ll need.

Ski level

A confident off-piste skier in steep and variable snow, who has toured before. Our Learn to Ski Tour trip counts.

Altitude

You don’t need to have been this high before, but a previous trek above 4,000m helps. The whole group is checked daily, including oxygen saturation.

Kit

Touring skis and skins, ski mountaineering boots that take crampons, transceiver, probe, shovel, harness and crampons. A full kit list comes with your booking.

The ski descent

The ski descent is never guaranteed. Many climbers summit Mera and walk down because the snow isn’t skiable that day. We plan every day around skiing, and the guides decide.

Questions

Asked, and answered honestly.

How technical is the climb?

Mera is a glacier climb rather than a technical one, with a short steeper section near the top where you’re roped to the guides. The challenge is altitude and effort, not rock or ice skills. You’ll learn everything you need at Khare.

Will I definitely ski from the summit?

No, and nobody honest will promise it. If the snow is wind-scoured or icy, the guides will have you walk the upper section and ski lower down. Every descent is judged on the day.

I’ve never been above 5,000m. Is that a problem?

Not if you’re fit and well prepared. The itinerary climbs slowly, with acclimatisation nights and a spare day. A previous trek above 4,000m, or our Learn to Ski Tour trip, is the best preparation.

Why October and April?

They’re Mera’s two climbing windows. October brings stable weather after the monsoon; April brings longer, warmer days before it. Both are good for skiing, in different ways.

What comes next?

Mera is the qualifying summit for our Himlung Himal Ski Expedition at 7,126m.
